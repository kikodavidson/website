The Plait Process

Six strands.
One funnel that holds.

A funnel breaks where it isn't connected. Messaging that doesn't match the creative. Hooks that don't match the offer. A site that betrays the ad. Most agencies tighten one or two strands and call it a day. I look at all six.

01
Messaging What you actually say to people
+

Most copy you've read online this year was written by a robot, or by someone using a robot. Yours won't be. Real messaging starts with what someone actually wants — and what they secretly want to be told.

  • Unique value propositions. The things only you can claim. We sharpen them until copying you would feel obvious.
  • Secondary and tertiary benefits. The buying reasons people don't admit out loud. This is where most brands leave money on the table.
  • Non-obvious connection points. The cultural moment, the rival product, the thing in their feed yesterday. Context is most of the message.
02
Creative The vehicle for the message
+

Strategy without creative is a deck no one reads. Creative without strategy is content for the sake of content. The job is to put the right thing in front of the right person on the right platform — and let the data sort it out.

  • Ideation. Hooks, angles, frameworks. I bring the concepts; the right creators bring them to life.
  • The right person creating it. A UGC creator who actually fits your audience hits different than a generic studio shoot. Casting matters more than production value.
  • The right format for the right platform. Meta favors tight, clean, polished. TikTok favors native, raw, UGC. Running the same creative on both is how you burn a budget.
  • Tested before scaled. No creative goes wide without earning it. We test with discipline and let the data pick winners.
  • Used to define audiences. In a post-targeting world, creative is the targeting. Different angles attract different buyer segments. We use them to map your market.
  • Built around Meta's Andromeda model. Meta's AI ad system rewards creative volume and variation. Most marketers haven't caught up. I have.
03
Hooks The first three seconds
+

You have three seconds. Maybe two. Hook fails, everything after it is dead spend. So we engineer the opening like a job interview where the interviewer is also scrolling Instagram.

  • First three seconds. Pattern interrupt, fast cut, or a sentence that demands the next one. Anything less and you're paying for an impression no one saw.
  • Audience callouts. "If you're a [specific person], stop scrolling." Done right, the wrong audience swipes away and your CPM drops on the right one.
  • Eye-catching visuals. Movement, contrast, unexpected color. The phone is fighting you. We fight back.
  • Bait-and-switch openings. Lead with something that has nothing to do with the product, then earn the pivot. Done well, it's one of the best-performing hook styles on the platform right now.
  • Questions. Implant a thought they want answered. Now they have to keep watching to get it.
  • Shock or surprise. Used sparingly. Used honestly. Earned attention only.
04
Offers The reason they actually click
+

Offer beats copy. Always has. The slickest creative in the world can't sell a bad deal, and a great deal can carry mediocre creative. We treat the offer like part of the product itself.

  • Product value. The actual offer architecture. Bundles, tiers, anchoring, urgency. The same product packaged five different ways will convert five different ways.
  • Discounts. Real ones. Not the kind where everyone knows it's always 30% off. Discount discipline protects margin and protects trust.
  • Promos. Seasonal, behavioral, retargeting-triggered. Promo strategy is its own discipline and most brands wing it.
  • Different value props for different segments. Cold traffic gets a different reason to buy than warm. Same product, different doorway.
05
CRO From click to checkout
+

Most brands obsess over getting the click. The click is the easy part. CRO is what happens between the ad and the credit card — and it's where most of your money is actually being lost.

  • Seamless traffic from LP to purchase. Every step audited for friction. Form fields, page load, checkout flow, payment options. We trim until it's invisible.
  • Education vs speed. Some products convert on impulse. Others need to be explained. Knowing which one you're selling decides everything about the funnel architecture.
06
Website The home base
+

Your site is the only thing in your funnel you fully control. Ads change. Algorithms change. Costs change. The site doesn't have to. It should be the most reliable salesperson on your team.

  • Design. First impression. A site that looks dated tells someone the product probably is too.
  • Professionalism. Trust signals, social proof, copy that doesn't read like a Mad Lib.
  • Education. The site has to explain what the ad couldn't. Long-form sections, FAQs, comparisons, demos — whatever the product needs.
  • Easy to navigate. Same principle as CRO. If they can't find what they want in two clicks, they leave.

Six strands. Tested individually, woven together, pulled tight. A funnel only holds if every section is connected — from first touch to purchase. That's the plait.
